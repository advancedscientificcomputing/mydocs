The cycle supports permanent adaptation under changing conditions.

I apply the same discipline through the Reverse Engineering Lifecycle. Reverse engineering is the systematic process of dissecting an existing product, system, or method to understand its design, structure, and function. It moves from finished artifact back to underlying logic so that weaknesses can be exposed and stronger versions can be built. The lifecycle follows seven stages: Research and Reconnaissance, Disassembly and Dissection, Analysis and Documentation, Redesign and Conceptualization, Rebuild and Implementation, Testing and Validation, and Redistribution and Release. Each stage is executed with clear mission definition, legal and ethical boundaries, clean-room methods, and measurable validation. The goal is not imitation. The goal is improved function, greater resilience, and open release of the result.
